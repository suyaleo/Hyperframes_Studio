# Hyperframes Studio — Visual Identity

## Style Prompt

Dense editorial motion graphics for evidence-backed briefings: a warm black canvas, paper-white Korean typography, restrained orange signals, precise rules, and quiet data texture. Frames should feel like a serious broadcast graphics package rather than a presentation template. Each aspect ratio preserves the same hierarchy while using its own spatial composition.

## Colors

- Canvas: `#0a0a0b`
- Raised panel: `#151311`
- Primary text: `#f4f1ea`
- Secondary text: `#c4bdb3`
- Signal orange: `#f15a24`
- Soft orange: `#ff8a57`

## Typography

- Korean display and body: `Pretendard`, falling back to `Apple SD Gothic Neo`
- Metadata and numeric labels: `SFMono-Regular`, falling back to `Menlo`
- Headlines use 780–900 weight; supporting copy uses 350–500 weight.

## Motion

- Medium editorial energy with decisive entrances and a short crossfade/push handoff.
- Build the most important text first, then structural rules and secondary evidence.
- Preserve readable holds; never animate all elements with the same direction or duration.

## Aspect Direction

- `9:16`: vertical story stack, large headline, lower evidence rail.
- `16:9`: asymmetric split frame with content and an evidence/index panel.
- `1:1`: compact editorial poster with a lower context panel.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not stretch or crop a portrait layout to fill landscape or square output.
- Do not leave a single text block floating in a large empty frame.
- Do not use neon blue/purple gradients, glass cards, or decorative dashboard grids.
- Do not reduce essential body text below 20 rendered pixels.
- Do not clip titles, bullets, citations, or safe-area metadata.
